[VML (Verse Markup Language)](https://github.com/nevmenandr/VML) is a minimalist, humanities-friendly markup language for the machine-readable representation of poetic texts. Unlike universal markup languages (HTML, XML, Markdown) or plain-text formats, VML focuses on the elements specific to poetry that matter for formal and quantitative analysis: it lets you explicitly mark up the author, titles and subtitles, epigraphs, verse lines, stanzas, dates and places of writing, poetic cycles, "ladder" (stepped) line layouts, foreign-language insertions, and basic metrical characteristics. The syntax is deliberately sparse — tags are short and do not clutter the poetic text, keeping it readable for humans, and the required tags are reduced to just two (`<a>` for the author and `<&>` for the incipit). The language is universal and not tied to any particular language of poetry (Russian, English, Chinese, etc.), and it is equally applicable to poems written by humans and by artificial intelligence, which makes it a convenient tool for comparative studies of human and generative poetics. It is designed above all for scholars in the traditional humanities without specialised computer training, and can serve as a way of bridging the gap between humanities researchers and the engineers involved in a digital project. The specification and reference parsers are distributed under the free Apache 2.0 licence, so anyone can freely use, modify and redistribute the language and its tools, including for commercial purposes.
